As of October 2026. Visual rules are in [`brand-guidelines.md`](brand-guidelines.md). Proof for any number you use is in [`proof-points.md`](proof-points.md). Quotes are in [`testimonials.md`](testimonials.md). Use US spelling throughout.

## Voice in one line

Precise, reliable, quietly confident: a tool that does exactly what it says, without fuss. **Not clinical. Not playful. Not startup-energetic.** Earned trust, clearly communicated.

The audience is procurement teams at manufacturers, carriers, builders and food producers. They want a tool that earns confidence, not one that performs excitement.

---

## Five adjectives

1. **Precise.** Specific numbers and named customers beat adjectives. "Live in 4 days, no IT team required," never "quick and easy."
2. **Direct.** Say it in the buyer's own words. "Stop chasing vendors for expired certificates" works because it is blunt and specific.
3. **Quietly confident.** State results plainly and let proof carry the weight. No hype, no exclamation marks, no "revolutionary."
4. **Respectful.** The buyer built the spreadsheet; honor that effort, then show where it breaks. Be honest when a competitor is the better fit.
5. **Grounded.** Concrete consequences (a stoppage at 7am, a lapsed SQF cert, a two-week scramble) over abstract risk language.

### Voice habits

- Lead with the customer's problem or result, then the product.
- Prove with a named customer and a number whenever possible.
- Short declarative sentences; concrete nouns.
- Use customer quotes for emotion and keep your own copy plain.
- Numbers over adjectives: "11 hours a week," "94% in 30 days," "9 days to 2."

### Vocabulary by vertical

"Vendor" is the default umbrella term in general copy, headings and the product. In vertical copy, lead with the local term:

| Vertical | Lead with |
|---|---|
| Manufacturing | suppliers |
| Food & beverage | suppliers |
| Logistics | carriers |
| Construction | subcontractors, or "subs" |
| Facilities management | vendors and subcontractors |

## Words and phrases we avoid

| Avoid | Write this instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Streamline your compliance workflow | Stop chasing vendors for expired certificates | Buyers say "chasing," not "streamline" |
| Vendor management software | Vendor compliance tracking tool | Puts us in the broad category with the wrong competitors |
| Compliance platform | Tool, or name the outcome | "Platform" implies a breadth we deliberately don't have |
| Vendor lifecycle management | (don't use) | Not the buyer's vocabulary |
| Centralize your vendor data | Vendors upload their own documents | Sounds like a migration project |
| Easy to implement / quick setup / fast time to value | Live in 4 days, no IT team required | Vague adjectives are claims; numbers are proof |
| Audit readiness | Pass your next audit without scrambling | Mirror the buyer's word |
| Improved compliance outcomes | 100% audit compliance after go-live | Specific beats vague |
| Proactive compliance management | Catch lapsed certificates before they cause a delay | Inside-out jargon |
| Compliance risk exposure | Gaps show up as project stoppages and audit findings | Concrete beats abstract |
| Legacy system | The spreadsheet works until it doesn't | Don't mock the buyer's spreadsheet |
| Disruptor, revolutionary, game-changing, cutting-edge, transform | (cut) | Signals startup energy |
| Fool-proof, never misses, zero effort, fully automatic | "The system runs itself; you handle the exceptions" | Absolutes overpromise; the product relies on people for exceptions |
| No IT, no IT ticket | "No code. Minimal IT support." (ERP) or "No IT team required" (core setup) | ERP setup typically needs 20–30 minutes of IT |
| Real-time sync | "Updates within 15–20 minutes" | Accuracy |
| "Vendor management" (to vendor contacts) | "Your customer's compliance checklist" | Vendors don't see themselves as managed |
| Hours saved as the lead for a COO | Risk avoided; cost of one incident | Wrong priority for that buyer |
| Feature names (CertVault, RenewalPulse) for COO audiences | Plain-language outcome | Meaningless to them |

**Also avoid:** exclamation marks, emoji in customer-facing copy, cursing, playful phrasing, and any number that isn't in `proof-points.md`.

**Tier labels.** When copy mentions RiskMap, escalation routing, configurable sequences or audit-ready PDF export, say "Professional" (or "Professional and Scale"). Mention SyncBridge, compliance-gated POs, API and SSO as "Scale." Because "scale" is also a common verb in our copy, avoid phrasing like "scale on Scale"; say "the Scale plan" when it could be ambiguous, and never call TraceDesk "enterprise-grade."

## Managing expectations around "runs itself"

"Runs itself" is figurative. Always make clear what runs and what a person still does:

- **Runs:** reminders at 90/60/30 days, vendor uploads, expiry tracking, escalation to the vendor's account owner.
- **A person handles:** the exceptions the dashboard flags, such as a vendor who ignores every reminder.

Use the support line: *Your vendors upload and renew their own certificates. You manage the exceptions.*

## Real examples to write from

These come from the 09/09/2026 one-pager (`tracedesk_onepager_09092026.pdf`) written by the team. It is the model for the voice.

### What the writing does well

**1. Headline plus one-sentence definition**

> **Stop chasing vendors for expired certificates**
> TraceDesk is the vendor compliance tracking tool that runs itself — built for procurement teams who've outgrown the spreadsheet.

The headline uses the buyer's words. The sentence under it names the category, the differentiator, the buyer and the status quo.

**2. Verb-led section headings built on a before-and-after pair**

> **Shift from chasing to managing**
> **Ready for an audit on any morning**
> **Vendors take ownership, you take back control**

Short, concrete and a little dry. "On any morning" is specific.

**3. Say the objection, then answer it with a number**

> Spreadsheets that depend on one person or institutional knowledge in order to function create a single point of failure in any system. […] While most procurement teams assume vendors won't bother, we've found that 94% of vendors are actively using the tool within 30 days.

Names the real pain and the real doubt before offering proof.

**4. A product line written as a plain observation**

> Your vendor roster and your ERP tell the same story — automatically. New vendors added in SAP, NetSuite, or Dynamics 365 appear in TraceDesk with no manual step, and compliance status writes back to your ERP vendor records.

Echoes the customer phrase "two systems, two stories" and describes what happens rather than naming a feature.

**5. A concrete call to action**

> **See your vendor list in TraceDesk in 30 minutes.**

A specific time and the buyer's own data. The 30-minute demo includes importing the prospect's vendor list (see `products.md`).

**6. Customer quotes carry the emotion**

Paul Bergman's "I used to chase vendors… Now I manage exceptions" and Derek Chu's "We were both right" do the emotional work, so the copy can stay plain.

### Where the writing drifts

For future drafts, these are the weaker patterns in the one-pager and their stronger replacements.

| One-pager line | Issue | Closer to the voice |
|---|---|---|
| "…without the hassle of manual admin" | Generic benefit | "Stop spending hours on reminder emails" |
| "…feel confident that nothing has been missed" | Abstract | "Know which vendors are about to lapse before an auditor asks" |
| "…ensuring peace of mind that no vendor or certificate has been missed" | Absolute and abstract | "You get told about the ones who need your attention" |
| "…proactively identify risks, catch issues before they happen" | "Proactively" is on the avoid list; the clauses repeat each other | "Catch lapsed certificates before they cause a delay" |
| "TraceDesk offers a robust alternative" | "Robust" is filler | "TraceDesk is live in 4 days, no IT team required" |
| "It's easy to onboard new team members" | Vague adjective | "Vendors upload their own documents; there's nothing for your team to chase" |
| "Vendors have historically high adoption rates" | Vague | "94% of vendors are on the portal within 30 days" |
| "Build a fool-proof system" | Absolute claim | "Build a system that doesn't depend on one person" |
| Label "AUTOMATIONS" | Feature-category word | "CHASING" or "RENEWALS" |
| "No code, no IT ticket" | Overstates ERP setup | "No code. Minimal IT support." |

**Pattern to notice:** the strongest lines are short and use customer words. The weaker ones are longer sentences that slip into general benefit language ("peace of mind," "robust," "proactively"). When in doubt, write the short version.

## Quick self-check before publishing

- Does it contain 2–3 of the top-ranked signal words?
- Is every claim a number or a named customer, not an adjective?
- Would a procurement manager recognize their own situation in the first sentence?
- Did we avoid the terms in the avoid table?
- Is every number in `proof-points.md`, with the right attribution (company-wide average versus named customer)?
- Are plan-specific features labeled with their plan?
